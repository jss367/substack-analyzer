# Phase 2 simulation outputs: one-time $1,000 spend in month 1

This note explains the two CSV files generated by running

```bash
python scripts/run_simulator.py --phase1 outputs/phase1.json --spend-once 1000
```

using the Phase 1 artifact included in `outputs/phase1.json`.

## `sim_once_1000_m1.csv`

This is the month-by-month operational projection that the simulator emits. Key sections of the file:

### Subscriber stocks
- `free_subscribers`, `premium_subscribers`, and `total_subscribers` track the active subscribers in each segment after all conversions and churn for the month have been applied. The first month’s totals reflect the one-time paid acquisition push *after* the carrying-capacity damping is applied, so the jump is much smaller than the previous unconstrained run.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L12-L16】
- Because the Phase 1 fit supplies a carrying capacity, the simulator now slows both organic and paid acquisition as the audience nears that ceiling. The totals therefore level off near the ~6,100-subscriber ceiling implied by the logistic fit instead of exploding without bound.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L17-L18】

### Monthly flows
- `new_free_organic` and `new_free_paid` represent the inbound free subscribers each month. Month 1’s paid-acquired joins are still derived from the $1,000 spend at a $2 CAC, but the carrying-capacity factor now dampens how many of those signups “stick.” From month 2 onward only the organic channel contributes, and those gains taper as the base approaches saturation.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L21-L25】
- `free_churned` shows cancellations among free readers; `premium_converted_from_new` and `premium_converted_from_existing` convert a slice of the free base into paying members; `premium_churned` removes paying subscribers. All conversions start at zero from the new-acquisition cohort and slowly increase as the free base scales, while churn stays very low relative to gross adds in this scenario.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L27-L30】

### Spend and fees
- `ad_spend` and `ad_manager_fee` capture marketing outlay. Only month 1 shows the $1,000 spend; subsequent months stay at zero because this is a single-shot campaign.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L33-L34】
- `cumulative_ad_spend` is therefore flat at $1,000 after the first month.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L35-L36】

### Revenue and profit
- `mrr_gross` and `mrr_net` express subscription revenue at the list price and after processor fees. `net_revenue` equals `mrr_net` in this configuration because there is no additional revenue component.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L39-L41】
- `profit` is monthly net revenue minus the ad spend and fee in that month. The carrying-capacity throttle keeps the premium base in line with the logistic fit so cumulative net profit now settles near the low-thousands of dollars by month 60 instead of ballooning unrealistically.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L36-L39】
- `cumulative_net_profit` aggregates profit over time so you can identify payback (month 2 in this case, when the cumulative line first crosses zero) and confirm that the totals remain consistent with the inferred ceiling.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L39-L41】

## `fitted_forecast.csv`

Because the Phase 1 artifact includes logistic fit parameters, the simulator also evaluates the Phase 1 piecewise logistic model under the same ad spend schedule.

- The `total_forecast` column provides the total-subscriber trajectory the logistic model expects at the same monthly cadence as the simulation. With the provided parameters it levels off near the carrying capacity of ~6,120 subscribers, so the series quickly asymptotes around 6,157 and stays there through the forecast horizon.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L51-L55】
- Comparing this to the scenario CSV now shows close agreement: the flow-based simulator respects the same ceiling that the logistic forecast encodes, while still exposing the granular conversions, churn, and revenue metrics missing from the fit alone.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L46-L48】

## How to use the outputs

- Use the simulation CSV when you want detailed operational metrics: conversions, churn, revenue, payback, and cumulative economics.
- Use the logistic `fitted_forecast.csv` when you want to check the projected total audience against the Phase 1 growth model or sanity-check whether the simulation is growing faster than the historical fit allows.

Taken together, these files let you track both the granular unit economics of a specific spend plan and how that plan compares to the macro subscriber ceiling inferred from historical data.【F:analysis/sim_once_1000_m1_explanation.md†L61-L64】
